\begin{abstract}
% ============================================================

We present the first full-scale temporal analysis of a national legal citation network: 345 million citation events from 33 million Ukrainian court decisions (2007--2026), decomposed into five event-aligned periods.
Using data-driven co-citation analysis on 18{,}000--29{,}500 legislation articles per period (50--102 million edges each), we uncover a three-phase structural dynamic validated by five independent methods.

\emph{Phase I -- Clarification} (2007--2019): community count declines while spectral separability \emph{increases} 14-fold (Fiedler $\lambda_2$: $0.0027 \to 0.0002$), indicating fewer but sharper legal domains.
\emph{Phase II -- Consolidation and disruption} (2020--2026): the pre-invasion period achieves near-perfect stability (NMI $= 0.96$), followed by the 2022 invasion producing the strongest structural shock of any transition (NMI $= 0.77$, $p < 10^{-4}$ vs.\ all others), splitting civil procedure and crystallizing a new administrative/misdemeanor domain.
\emph{Phase III -- Reorganization}: the wartime $k = 5$ partition stabilizes rapidly (within-period NMI $= 0.94$) and captures citation-practice distinctions invisible to semantic embeddings (co-citation vs.\ LLM NMI: $0.71$ at peak, diverging to $0.43$ under war).

Five validations confirm these findings: formal jurisdiction alignment (NMI $0.46$--$0.71$), sub-period stability, bootstrap significance tests, spectral analysis, and LLM embedding comparison.
Results are robust to resolution, threshold, and period boundary ($\pm 1$ year) variation.

\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} temporal networks, legal citation graph, community evolution, spectral analysis, text embeddings, Ukrainian courts, war impact, EDRSR

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

Legal citation networks encode the operational structure of a legal system: which norms courts actually apply, how frequently, and in what combinations.
Prior work has constructed such networks for the US Supreme Court~\citep{fowler2007network}, Dutch case law~\citep{winkels2011determining}, Danish courts~\citep{mones2021emergence}, and French legal codes~\citep{mazzega2009network}.
These studies treat citation networks as static objects.
The temporal dimension -- how citation patterns evolve in response to reform, crisis, and conflict -- remains largely unexplored at scale.

The companion paper~\citep{ovcharov2026citationgraph} constructed the first large-scale legal citation graph from the complete Ukrainian court record: 502 million citation edges from 101 million decisions.
That analysis was static.
This paper develops the temporal dimension and introduces two methodological advances: \emph{(i)}~data-driven node selection that eliminates the survivorship bias of fixed top-$k$ approaches, and \emph{(ii)}~multi-method validation combining graph-theoretic, statistical, and semantic analyses.

We decompose the citation record into five event-aligned periods spanning two decades of Ukrainian legal history: the pre-Maidan baseline (2007--2013), post-revolution reforms (2014--2016), judicial modernization (2017--2019), pre-invasion stability (2020--2021), and the full-scale Russian invasion (2022--2026).

Four research questions guide the analysis:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item[\textbf{RQ1}:] Does co-citation community structure change in response to legislative reform?
  \item[\textbf{RQ2}:] How fast do courts adopt new legislation, and does speed vary by region?
  \item[\textbf{RQ3}:] Does community structure evolve through gradual drift or discrete shocks?
  \item[\textbf{RQ4}:] What structural signature does the 2022 invasion leave on the citation network?
\end{enumerate}

Our principal finding is a three-phase dynamic: \emph{clarification} (fewer but spectrally sharper communities, P1--P3), \emph{consolidation and disruption} (maximum stability then maximum shock, P3--P5), and \emph{reorganization} into a new five-domain structure that stabilizes rapidly.
The invasion is the strongest structural shock ($p < 10^{-4}$) -- a finding that was masked by fixed-node analyses and is confirmed by five independent validation methods.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ============================================================

\citet{holme2012temporal} review temporal networks; we adopt the interval-graph approach with period-specific co-citation networks.
\citet{coupette2021measuring} track structural properties of US and German legal networks over time (${\sim}10^4$ nodes) but do not analyze co-citation community evolution.
\citet{bommarito2010mathematical} study the US Code as a citation network.
Neither work operates at the scale of $10^8$ decisions or combines community detection with spectral and embedding validation.

Spectral analysis of network evolution follows~\citet{newman2004finding} on modularity and~\citet{fiedler1973algebraic} on algebraic connectivity.
Our use of the Fiedler value as a detection-independent separability measure complements partition-based metrics (NMI, ARI).

The adoption of new legislation by courts can be modeled as a diffusion process~\citep{rogers1962diffusion}.
To our knowledge, no prior work has measured legislative adoption curves from court citation data at national scale.

\subsection{Temporal Co-Citation}

For each period $t$, we include all legislation articles cited by at least $\tau \cdot L_t$ decisions ($\tau = 5$, $L_t$ = period years).
This data-driven pre-filter is principled: articles below this threshold cannot generate co-citation weight $\geq \tau$ with any article.
Unlike fixed top-$k$ selection, it adapts to each period and avoids survivorship bias (Figure~\ref{fig:node_dynamics}).

We construct a sparse binary matrix $M^{(t)} \in \{0,1\}^{n_t \times k_t}$ and compute the co-citation matrix $C^{(t)} = (M^{(t)})^\top M^{(t)}$, normalized by period length: $\hat{C}^{(t)}_{ab} = C^{(t)}_{ab} / L_t$.
The upper triangle with entries $\geq \tau$ defines the co-citation network (Table~\ref{tab:cocitation}).

\subsection{Community Detection}

We apply Leiden~\citep{traag2019louvain} with 30 consensus runs ($\gamma = 1.0$, modularity maximization).
All runs converge to the same partition ($\sigma_Q < 10^{-4}$).
Modularity significance is confirmed via degree-preserving null models (all $z > 18$).

Community evolution is tracked via NMI and ARI on shared active nodes (18{,}094--28{,}989 per transition), with 1{,}000-iteration bootstrap confidence intervals.

\subsection{Validation Methods}

We apply five independent validation approaches:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Formal jurisdiction}: NMI between communities and the five-class \emph{justice\_kind} metadata.
  \item \emph{Sub-period stability}: split P5 into 2022--2023 and 2024--2026; compare partitions.
  \item \emph{Statistical significance}: pairwise bootstrap comparison of NMI across transitions.
  \item \emph{Spectral analysis}: Fiedler value $\lambda_2$ of the normalized Laplacian as a detection-independent separability measure.
  \item \emph{Semantic embeddings}: $k$-means clustering of multilingual LLM embeddings (intfloat/multilingual-e5-large, 1{,}024 dims) compared with co-citation communities.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Phase I: Clarification (RQ1, RQ3)}

The effective community count declines from $k_{\text{eff}} = 4$ (P1; raw $k=8$ includes 4 micro-clusters of 2 nodes) through $k=4$ (P3) to $k=3$ (P4), as the commercial appeals sub-community merges with commercial procedure and civil domains consolidate (Table~\ref{tab:domains}, Figure~\ref{fig:alluvial}).

Crucially, this is not mere coarsening.
The Fiedler value $\lambda_2$ \emph{decreases} from $0.0027$ (P1) to $0.0002$ (P3) -- a 14$\times$ drop (Table~\ref{tab:cocitation}) -- indicating that the remaining communities become progressively \emph{more separable}, not less.
At P3, the ratio $\lambda_2/\lambda_3 = 0.02$ indicates a strong spectral gap: the graph has a well-defined bipartition far more pronounced than any subsequent split.
This is \emph{clarification}: fewer domains, each spectrally sharper.

Transition stability confirms gradualism: NMI increases monotonically from $0.826$ (P1$\to$P2) to $0.960$ (P3$\to$P4; Table~\ref{tab:nmi}), with the pre-invasion period achieving near-perfect partition preservation.

P1 warrants methodological caution: density $\rho = 0.33$ and modularity barely above null ($Q = 0.027$, $Q_{\text{null}} = 0.026$).
The 7-year window inflates edge count despite weight normalization.

Of the 18{,}338 shared nodes, 5{,}781 (31.5\%) change community.
Three flows account for 98\% of movement:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Civil procedure $\to$ admin/misdemeanor} (2{,}293 nodes): CAO, APC, and court fee articles separate into a distinct cluster incorporating wartime administrative law.
  \item \emph{Civil procedure $\to$ cassation} (2{,}240 nodes): cassation articles (CPC\textsubscript{civ} art.~392, 447) separate from first-instance procedure.
  \item \emph{Criminal intact} (1{,}141 nodes): the criminal domain moves as a coherent block.
\end{itemize}

At the individual-law level, martial law citations grew from 3 in 2021 to 12{,}136 in 2022 (4{,}000$\times$); mobilization grew to 32{,}209 by 2025 (Figure~\ref{fig:war_adoption}).

The spectral signature confirms disruption: $\lambda_2$ jumps 50$\times$ from $0.0002$ (P3) to $0.0095$ (P4), indicating that the consolidation destroyed spectral separability.
P5 shows $\lambda_2 = 0.0112$ -- the five new communities exist but remain spectrally soft.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Three phases, not ``stable resilience.''}
Fixed-node analyses assigned NMI $= 0.81$ to the invasion transition -- higher than the reform transition -- suggesting the war was less disruptive.
The full-scale analysis reverses this.
The discrepancy arises because wartime articles, absent from the aggregate top-5{,}000, are precisely the nodes driving structural change.
Data-driven node selection allows 1{,}000--11{,}000 new articles per period (Figure~\ref{fig:node_dynamics}), eliminating this bias.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\input{figures/fig13_node_dynamics.tex}
\caption{Node set dynamics. Blue: shared with previous period. Red: newly entering.}
\label{fig:node_dynamics}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Spectral clarification precedes consolidation.}
The spectral trajectory reveals that consolidation has two distinct stages: first \emph{clarification} (P1--P3, $\lambda_2$ drops 14$\times$, communities sharpen), then \emph{merger} (P3--P4, $\lambda_2$ jumps 50$\times$, boundaries dissolve).
This distinction is invisible to community count alone ($k = 4 \to 3$) and requires the algebraic connectivity measure to detect.

\paragraph{Citation practice vs.\ semantic similarity.}
The P5 divergence between co-citation ($k=5$) and embedding ($k=3$) clusters is not a limitation but a finding: wartime conditions create citation-practice domains (first-instance vs.\ cassation procedure) that are semantically indistinguishable (both are ``civil procedure'') but functionally distinct in court practice.
This has implications for legal information retrieval systems that rely on text similarity -- they would miss these practice-based boundaries.

\paragraph{NMI on shared nodes.}
NMI excludes newly entering articles, systematically underestimating disruption.
The reported NMI $= 0.77$ is a lower bound on similarity among \emph{continuing} articles; the true structural change, including new wartime legislation, is larger.

\paragraph{Entropy paradox.}
Community reorganization (NMI $= 0.77$) coexists with distributional stability (no entropy anomaly).
Nodes move between domains, but newly important wartime articles receive citation shares comparable to those they replace.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Coverage gaps in 2008--2009 reduce P1 reliability.
OCR artifacts affect pre-2010 extraction.
Unequal period durations give later periods more statistical power despite normalization.
The embedding validation uses article identifiers, not full text; full-text embeddings would provide stronger semantic comparison.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

Analyzing 345 million citation events across five periods with data-driven node selection, spectral analysis, and LLM embedding validation, we find:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Clarification}: community count declines while spectral separability increases 14$\times$ (P1--P3). Fewer domains, each sharper.
  \item \textbf{Disruption}: the 2022 invasion is the strongest shock ($p < 10^{-4}$), splitting civil procedure and creating admin/misdemeanor. Sub-period analysis confirms rapid stabilization (within-P5 NMI $= 0.94$).
  \item \textbf{Validation}: five independent methods confirm the three-phase dynamic. Formal jurisdiction alignment tracks the cycle ($0.71 \to 0.46 \to 0.59$). Spectral analysis provides detection-independent confirmation. LLM embeddings reveal that co-citation captures practice-based boundaries invisible to semantics.
  \item \textbf{Robustness}: stable across $\gamma \in [0.5, 1.0]$, threshold $\in [3, 10]$, and boundary $\pm 1$ year (NMI identical to 3 decimal places).
\end{enumerate}

The co-citation networks, community assignments, and analysis code are released as open data.
